Skeptic Q&A

Answer short, then offer to show it.

"Affinity or DealCloud already shows me warm intros."

Keep them; a path is one signal. Affinity knows who you know. It does not know Cedar Row Partners is running Larkspur Revenue Partners, Harbor Lane Advisors is engaged on Saltmarsh Home Infusion, or that Tessmark Clinical Supply takes you over your Specialty Distribution guideline. Here each path is weighted by tenure overlap and recency, false ties are rejected, and access is combined with fit, readiness and conflicts in one governed score. Affinity and PitchBook become inputs, not rivals.

"Half your Tier A list already has a banker. That's not sourcing."

Right, and the model knew a banker was engaged months before a teaser would reach you. The value is position: who on our team knows that banker (the warm-path act) and whether we are on their call list (the deal-flow act).

"DealCloud already has a scoring field."

A field an associate types is stale by Friday. This score is re-derived from dated facts every run, with the reason attached.

"Grata would have found Saltmarsh Home Infusion."

Grata finds companies. It does not rank them by readiness and access with evidence, or check them against your portfolio.

"Where does debt maturity come from on a founder-owned company?"

Vendor debt data for sponsor-backed names, lender and banker conversations logged in the CRM, UCC filings. Where you do not have it, the point is not awarded, and the scorecard shows that.

"Does our deal data leave Snowflake?"

The data, the model and the reasoning stay in your account. Claude sees the results of the queries it runs, the answer rows on screen, not your tables. If those rows must stay inside the boundary too, Claude models are available inside Snowflake through Cortex.

From the domain brief:

"Our CRM data is a mess. Garbage in, garbage out."

Each score shows its components and evidence, so missing data appears as a missing component, not a silent zero. Start with three sources already in Snowflake: CRM, deal history, a vendor feed.

"Would I bet a deal on a score?"

No. It ranks who to call Monday and why; the rules are business language in your Snowflake, editable in a sentence. The opposite of a black box.
